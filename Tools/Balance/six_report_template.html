What changed

Specialists are faster individually and materially more expensive. Compared with binary-v3, structural/electrical/fluid construction costs are 4×, mechanical costs 6×, and industrial-smelter costs 3×. Specialist recipe times are 37.5% of v3; other normal recipe, construction and research times are 75%. Miner and fuel rates are correspondingly faster. Emergency recovery is unchanged.

The Bay and Fighter require 3× their previous phase materials, while their fixed phase durations are 25% shorter. The larger project bill competes with factory expansion. This is a substantial rebalance of the whole campaign, not just a price increase on the seventh machine. No count cap, escalating per-copy price, or artificial throughput ceiling was added.

The best tested factory ends with 6 structural, 4 electrical, 6 mechanical, 2 fluid machines and 6 industrial smelters. Mining uses 3 industrial iron, 2 copper, 1 coal and 1 silicate miner. Three industrial generators support it; two starter generators remain available. Sixty belt tiles represent two pooled input lanes per family.

Still short of earlier targets: the minimum-to-expanded gap is approximately 2.6×, not the requested 3×, and the fast route is roughly seven minutes above two hours. This pass prioritizes the six-machine investment window. At the reference resolution, five, six and seven structural/mechanical machines finish within about 16 seconds. At 0.0625-second ticks, six takes 127.77 minutes and seven 127.80 minutes: effectively tied. Six is the measured winner, but there is no meaningful penalty at exactly seven; the penalty becomes clearer at eight and twelve.

What the simulation does—and does not—prove

The planner explicitly pauses idle or excess consumers to fit available power. Both compared routes receive that same control. It pays construction costs and waits through research, material production, recipe changes, fuel use, and project phases. Transfers obey pooled belt input and machine-output budgets; physical belt geometry and player placement effort are not simulated. Construction and final-project cargo delivery remain optimistic.

The leading route is best among tested policies, not a global optimum. These reference runs use 0.125-second ticks, with 0.25-second and 0.0625-second cross-checks of the leading route and close alternatives. Additional tests raised one family at a time, reordered industrial startup, and delayed extra machines until later research. Different future goals can justify more than six. Full building reclamation remains available; arbitrary sell/rebuild schedules were not searched exhaustively.
